# The Aisle Stack AI Toolkit
### 10 ready-to-use AI prompts for independent wedding & event planners

How to use this pack: copy a prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, or any equivalent AI assistant, replace everything in [brackets] with your real details, and run it. Each prompt includes a short note on what to check before sending the output to a client — the AI drafts, you are still the professional signing off on it.

---

## 1. Discovery call recap and proposal draft

```
You are a senior wedding/event planner writing a proposal recap after a discovery call.

Client name: [client name]
Event type: [wedding / corporate / birthday / other]
Date and location: [date, city/venue if known]
Guest count: [number]
Budget range discussed: [range]
Style/vibe keywords from the call: [3-5 words the client used]
Must-haves mentioned: [list]
Concerns or hesitations mentioned: [list]

Write a warm, professional recap email that:
1. Thanks them for their time and reflects back their vision in their own words
2. Summarizes what you understood about scope, date, and budget range
3. Lists the next 3 concrete steps with a rough timeline
4. Closes with one specific question that moves the decision forward

Keep it under 250 words. No generic wedding-industry clichés ("your special day," "dream come true").
```
**Check before sending:** confirm every number (budget, guest count, date) matches your notes exactly — the AI cannot verify facts it wasn't given correctly.

## 2. Full service proposal document

```
Draft a service proposal for a [event type] planning client.

Package tier: [full planning / partial planning / day-of coordination]
Price: [amount]
What's included: [list your real deliverables]
Payment schedule: [e.g., 30% deposit, 40% at 90 days out, 30% at 30 days out]
Timeline from booking to event: [X months]

Structure the proposal with these sections: Overview, What's Included, What's Not Included, Timeline, Investment, Payment Schedule, Next Steps. Write in a confident, warm tone — this is a document the client will re-read before deciding, not a sales pitch they skim once.
```
**Check before sending:** legal/contract terms (cancellation policy, liability) should come from your actual contract template, not be generated fresh by the AI each time.

## 3. Vendor negotiation email

```
Write a negotiation email to a [vendor type: florist / caterer / photographer / venue] on behalf of a client.

Vendor's initial quote: [amount]
Client's budget for this line item: [amount]
What flexibility exists: [e.g., can move date, can reduce guest count for catering, can accept a less popular time slot]
Relationship context: [first time working with this vendor / repeat vendor]

Write a firm but collaborative email that proposes a specific adjusted scope or price, explains the reasoning in one sentence, and leaves room for the vendor to counter. Do not apologize for negotiating — this is a normal, respectful part of vendor relationships.
```
**Check before sending:** never let the AI invent a "competitor quote" or a false urgency claim — negotiate on real constraints only.

## 4. Day-of timeline builder

```
Build a minute-by-minute day-of timeline for a [event type].

Ceremony time: [time]
Reception time: [time]
Guest count: [number]
Key moments to include: [first look, cocktail hour, speeches, first dance, cake cutting, send-off, etc. — list what applies]
Vendor call times needed: [list vendors: photographer, DJ, caterer, florist]
Venue load-in/load-out constraints: [any hard deadlines from the venue]

Output a timeline table with columns: Time | Activity | Who's involved | Notes. Build in 15-minute buffers around anything involving guest movement (ceremony seating, transitions between spaces).
```
**Check before sending:** cross-check every vendor call time against what you've actually confirmed with each vendor — the AI is scheduling logic, not a source of truth on vendor availability.

## 5. Client complaint / difficult conversation response

```
Help me respond to a client concern.

What the client said or complained about: [paraphrase or paste their message]
What actually happened: [your honest account]
What you can offer: [refund, credit, extra service, or nothing if the complaint isn't valid — be honest with yourself here]

Write a response that acknowledges their feeling first, states the facts calmly without being defensive, and proposes a specific resolution or explains clearly why no change will be made. No corporate hedging language ("we take this very seriously").
```
**Check before sending:** this is the highest-stakes prompt in the pack — read the output twice for tone before sending, and never send an AI-drafted apology for something you don't actually believe went wrong.

## 6. Client onboarding email sequence (3 emails)

```
Write a 3-email onboarding sequence for a new client who just signed a contract for [package tier].

Email 1 (same day as signing): welcome, confirm next steps, set expectation for response times
Email 2 (1 week later): request the information you need to start planning (style inspiration, guest list draft, must-have vendors)
Email 3 (2 weeks later): share a rough planning timeline so they know what happens when

Each email under 150 words. Warm but efficient — this client already said yes, the goal now is confidence, not persuasion.
```
**Check before sending:** adjust the request list in Email 2 to match your actual intake process.

## 7. Social media caption batch (event marketing)

```
Write 5 Instagram captions for real event photos, for a wedding/event planning business account.

Event type: [wedding / corporate / other]
Notable details worth mentioning: [venue, unusual detail, a real number like guest count or setup time]
Call to action: [book a call / DM for availability / link in bio]

Each caption: one specific, concrete detail from the real event (not generic praise), 1-2 sentences, ending with the call to action. No more than one emoji per caption, if any.
```
**Check before sending:** never publish a client's event details or photos without their permission, regardless of how good the caption is.

## 8. Referral / past-client re-engagement email

```
Write a short email to a past client, 6-12 months after their event, asking for a referral.

Their event: [type, rough date]
One specific detail you remember about their event: [detail]
What you're asking for: [a referral, a Google review, or both]

Keep it under 100 words, reference the specific detail so it doesn't read as a template, and make the ask a single clear sentence.
```
**Check before sending:** the "specific detail" must be real and correct — a wrong detail undoes all the warmth of the email.

---

## 9. Budget breakdown explainer for clients

```
Explain a budget breakdown to a client who is surprised by a cost.

Total budget: [amount]
Line item in question: [e.g., florals, catering, photography]
Cost of that line item: [amount]
Why it costs what it costs: [real reasons: labor hours, seasonal pricing, vendor minimums, etc.]
Alternative options if they want to reduce this cost: [list 1-3 real alternatives]

Write a clear, non-defensive explanation that states the number first, then the reason, then the alternatives if they want to adjust. No guilt-tripping language about "what they're getting for the price."
```
**Check before sending:** every number here should trace back to a real vendor quote — the AI should never fill in a plausible-sounding cost you haven't actually verified.

---

## 10. Post-event thank-you and review request

```
Write a thank-you email to a client 3-5 days after their event.

One specific moment from the event that went well: [detail]
Review platform to request: [Google / The Knot / WeddingWire]
Anything you're still following up on: [final invoice, vendor payments, photo delivery timeline]

Keep it under 120 words: genuine thanks referencing the specific moment, one clear review request with a direct link placeholder, and a note on any outstanding item so nothing feels dropped.
```
**Check before sending:** confirm you actually have permission/a working link to the review platform before sending.
