# The ClassStack AI Toolkit
### 10 ready-to-use AI prompts for independent tutors and private teachers

How to use this pack: copy a prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, or any equivalent AI assistant, replace everything in [brackets] with your real details, and run it. Each prompt includes a short note on what to check before sending the output to a parent or student — the AI drafts, you are still the professional signing off on it.

---

## 1. New student intake recap

```
You are an experienced private tutor writing a recap after a first meeting with a new student's parent.

Student name and age/grade: [name, grade/level]
Subject(s): [subject]
Goals discussed: [what the parent/student wants to achieve]
Current challenges mentioned: [list]
Availability discussed: [days/times]
Rate and session length: [rate, duration]

Write a warm, professional recap email that:
1. Thanks them for the conversation and reflects back the goals in their own words
2. Confirms the plan for the first few sessions
3. States the rate, schedule, and cancellation policy clearly
4. Closes with a specific next step (first session date, or what materials to prepare)

Keep it under 200 words. No generic tutoring-industry filler ("unlock your child's potential").
```
**Check before sending:** confirm the rate and cancellation policy match what you actually quoted — this email often becomes the reference point if there's a dispute later.

## 2. Session recap for parents

```
Write a short recap email to a parent after today's tutoring session.

Student name: [name]
Subject/topic covered: [topic]
What went well: [specific detail]
What needs more practice: [specific detail]
Homework or practice assigned: [what, and due when]

Keep it under 120 words, lead with something specific and positive, name the one area to work on clearly, and end with the homework assignment. No vague praise like "great job today" without a specific reason.
```
**Check before sending:** the "specific detail" must be true to what actually happened in the session — parents notice generic recaps quickly, especially after a few weeks.

## 3. Lesson plan generator

```
Build a lesson plan for a single tutoring session.

Subject and topic: [subject, specific topic]
Student level: [grade/skill level]
Session length: [minutes]
Student's learning style or preferences, if known: [e.g., visual, needs frequent breaks, responds well to real-world examples]
Goal for this specific session: [one clear goal]

Structure the plan with: warm-up (5 min), main teaching block with 2-3 concrete activities, a check for understanding, and a wrap-up with the homework assignment. Include actual example problems or discussion questions, not just activity names.
```
**Check before sending:** verify any factual content (formulas, historical dates, grammar rules) the AI includes — tutoring is exactly the context where a subtle factual error does real damage.

## 4. Difficult conversation with a parent

```
Help me write a message to a parent about a concern with their child's progress.

What the concern is: [e.g., student isn't completing homework, isn't improving despite consistent effort, behavior issue during sessions]
What you've already tried: [list]
What you're asking the parent to do: [specific, actionable ask]

Write a message that states the concern factually and without blame, acknowledges the student's effort where genuine, and makes one clear, specific ask of the parent. No hedging language that buries the actual concern.
```
**Check before sending:** this is the highest-stakes prompt in the pack — read it twice for tone, and make sure every claim about the student is something you can back up with a specific example.

## 5. End-of-term progress report

```
Write a formal end-of-term progress report for a tutoring student.

Student name: [name]
Subject: [subject]
Starting point at term start: [brief description]
Progress made: [specific, measurable where possible: grades, skills mastered, confidence]
Areas still developing: [list]
Recommendation for next term: [continue at same pace / increase frequency / specific new focus]

Structure: Summary, Progress This Term, Areas for Continued Growth, Recommendation. Professional tone, specific enough that a parent could not mistake it for a template filled in for any student.
```
**Check before sending:** every claim of measurable progress (grade change, specific skill) should be something you actually tracked, not something that sounds plausible.

## 6. Rescheduling and cancellation policy reminder

```
Write a polite but firm message reminding a parent of the cancellation policy, after a late cancellation or no-show.

What happened: [e.g., cancelled with 2 hours notice, no-show with no message]
Your policy: [e.g., 24-hour notice required, late cancellations charged at full rate]
Whether you're charging this time or making an exception: [state which, and why if making an exception]

Write a message that states what happened factually, restates the policy once (not defensively), and states clearly whether this session is being charged. Keep it short — this is a policy reminder, not a negotiation.
```
**Check before sending:** decide whether you're charging BEFORE writing the message — the AI should reflect your decision, not help you avoid making it.

## 7. Referral request to a satisfied parent

```
Write a short email to a parent whose child has been tutoring with you for [X months/terms], asking for a referral.

Specific progress this student has made: [detail]
What you're asking for: [a referral to another family, a testimonial, or both]

Keep it under 100 words, reference the specific progress so it doesn't read as a template, and make the ask a single clear sentence.
```
**Check before sending:** the progress detail must be accurate and something the parent would recognize immediately as true.

## 8. Social media caption batch

```
Write 5 social media captions for a tutoring business account.

Subject(s) you teach: [subject]
Recent win worth mentioning (anonymized): [e.g., a student's grade improvement, a milestone reached, without naming the student]
Call to action: [book a consultation / DM for availability / link in bio]

Each caption: one specific, concrete detail (anonymized, never identifying a real student), 1-2 sentences, ending with the call to action. No more than one emoji per caption, if any.
```
**Check before sending:** never include a real student's name, school, or identifying details, even with permission implied — anonymize every example.

## 9. Homework assignment with parent instructions

```
Create a homework assignment for a tutoring student, with instructions for the parent.

Subject and topic: [subject, topic]
Student level: [grade/level]
Time expected: [minutes]
What the student should be able to do after completing it: [specific skill/outcome]

Output two parts: (1) the assignment itself, with actual problems or tasks, and (2) a short note to the parent explaining what to check for and how much help is appropriate to give (e.g., "let them attempt problems 1-3 alone before helping").
```
**Check before sending:** verify every problem or question is actually correct and age-appropriate before sending it home.

---

## 10. New inquiry response (first contact)

```
Write a response to a parent who just inquired about tutoring for the first time.

Subject requested: [subject]
Student grade/level: [level]
Information they provided: [whatever they mentioned in their inquiry]
Your availability: [general availability]
Your rate: [rate]

Write a warm, professional response that answers their specific question, states your rate and availability clearly, and proposes one concrete next step (a call, a trial session, or a form to fill out). Keep it under 150 words — this is a first impression, not a full proposal.
```
**Check before sending:** confirm your stated availability is actually still accurate before sending — nothing undermines a first impression like offering a slot you no longer have.
